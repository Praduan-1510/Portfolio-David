Color

Brass, and what it ages into.

A meridian is a surveyor's line, so the palette is what a surveying instrument is made of: a machined slate housing, a brass index arm, and the oxides brass turns into as it weathers. Where the category defaults to corporate blue, this is a set of materials rather than hues: every colour sits in a narrow chroma band (C* 14–49, against 46–97 for a spectrum palette) and the ground is all but neutral (C* 1.4–4.5), so the metal reads as something sitting on a surface instead of light glowing out of one. Five washes, each staying inside one metal — about thirty degrees of tonal travel, never a jump between hues. The primary action is deliberately not among them: it is flat brass with the label engraved into it.

Slate neutrals

Machined slate, near-neutral by measurement: C* 1.4–4.5, down from the 6–30 of a violet-tinted ink. The cast is gone on purpose — brass has to sit on this, not glow out of it.

Semantic roles

Vibrant, but always paired with an icon or label: color is never the sole carrier of meaning.
