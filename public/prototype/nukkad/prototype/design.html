Nukkad design system

Everything, a corner away

One idea holds this together: edge-of-glass teal. Float glass is clear face-on but glows green-teal at its cut edge, so the brand colour is the material's own edge colour. It also lands in the one hue band no Indian quick-commerce app occupies — Zepto owns violet, Blinkit yellow, Instamart orange, Zomato red, BigBasket lime.

The mark

A lowercase n drawn as one road that turns a corner. The right leg stops short and a dot completes it: the destination, just around the corner. The gap between them is half a stroke wide, and that gap is the last minute of the delivery. Two shapes, one colour — which is why it survives at 16px and under Android's monochrome theming.

Colour

Peacock leads. Genda means exactly one thing everywhere in the app — money saved — and kulhad belongs to Tapri. Light mode is warm paper, never pure white; dark mode is teal-cast ink, never pure black.

Type

Bricolage Grotesque carries numbers and headlines, IBM Plex Sans carries words, and Plex Devanagari carries Hindi so a bilingual line does not look like a fallback. Deliberately not Inter, Poppins or Manrope — the default stack of generated interfaces.

Liquid Glass

Glass belongs to the floating control layer and nowhere else. Putting it on product cards is the fastest way to look like generic glassmorphism, so the content layer here stays opaque and the chrome does the refracting. Chromium gets a real SVG displacement filter inside backdrop-filter; Safari and Firefox get blur and saturation; Reduce Transparency gets an opaque surface.

Icons

The marks that carry meaning are drawn for Nukkad: a shopfront with a half-rolled shutter, a jaali screen with one cell lit, a cloth jhola, a door nameplate, and a bolt whose lower half turns ninety degrees — the corner.

Product art

Eighty-six packs, all generated from source. Each is a silhouette archetype chosen by shelf (carton, sack, tin, tube, blister), a hue keyed to the brand, and a contact shadow. Open packshot databases do cover Indian SKUs, but a mix of studio shots and phone photos taken on kitchen floors reads as scraped — one illustrated system reads as a shop with a shelf standard.
